# Chapter 62: Connective Tissue Disorders — Revision Notes

> Williams Obstetrics 26e, pp. 2888–2932 of the PDF. High-yield numbers are in **bold**.
> The seven tables are transcribed from the PDF images (they are missing from the extracted chapter text). This chapter has no figures.

**Big picture:** Connective tissue disorders are either **immune-complex (rheumatic) diseases** — SLE, RA, scleroderma, vasculitis, myopathies — or **inherited structural defects** — Marfan, Ehlers-Danlos, osteogenesis imperfecta. The immunosuppression of pregnancy (Th2 shift, progesterone) quiets some diseases (RA), while others flare unpredictably (SLE). The single most important predictor of a good outcome is **quiescent disease for ≥6 months before conception**. **APS** is the key acquired thrombophilia behind stillbirth, early preeclampsia and FGR.

---

## 1. Immune-Mediated Connective Tissue Diseases

- **Two groups:**
  - **With autoantibodies (rheumatoid factor–associated):** SLE, RA, systemic sclerosis, mixed CTD, dermatomyositis, polymyositis, vasculitides.
  - **RF-seronegative spondyloarthropathies (HLA-B27):** ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis, Reiter disease.
- **Why pregnancy can mitigate disease:** estrogens upregulate and androgens downregulate T cells; **progesterone is immunosuppressive**; **Th2 predominance** over Th1.
- **Unrecognized systemic rheumatic disease (~0.3%)** → **preeclampsia or FGR in 25%**.
- **Fetal microchimerism:** fetal cells and cfDNA persist in maternal blood/organs and may trigger autoantibodies (seen in autoimmune thyroiditis, **systemic sclerosis**, SLE, RA). Engrafted maternal cells may also provoke autoimmunity in offspring.

## 2. Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)

### Pathogenesis and genetics
- Tissue-binding autoantibodies and immune complexes against **nuclear components** + impaired regulatory T-cell function. Autoantibodies usually precede clinical disease by years.
- **Concordance: monozygotic 25% vs dizygotic 2%**; **10%** if one family member affected. Genes: chromosome 16 "autoimmunity gene", HLA-DR3, STAT4, APOL1, FCGR3A, DNAse1.
- A child's HLA-DRB1 genotype can raise maternal SLE risk. Neonatal lupus occurred even after **oocyte donation** to an anti-Ro/La-positive mother (antibody-mediated).

### Clinical manifestations and diagnosis
- **~90% of cases in women**; more common in African-American women. **~1 in 500** women of reproductive age.
- Variable presentation: may start in one organ or be multisystem. Common: malaise, fever, **arthritis, rash, pleuropericarditis, photosensitivity**, anemia, cognitive dysfunction. **≥ Half have renal involvement.**

  - Persistent unexplained ↑ **D-dimer → high thrombosis risk**.
- **Drug-induced lupus:** PPIs, thiazides, antifungals, antihypertensives, chemotherapy, statins, antiepileptics. **Rarely glomerulonephritis**; regresses when the drug stops (antihistone antibodies).

## 3. Lupus and Pregnancy

- **~1 in 900 births** (meta-analysis of ~9 million pregnancies).
- **Rule of thirds: improves in ⅓, unchanged in ⅓, worsens in ⅓** — flares can occur without warning.
- **Maternal mortality 325 per 100,000** (13,555 pregnancies); deaths occur almost only with **active disease**.
- ↑ Thrombosis, infection, PPH, transfusion.

|---|---|
| Preeclampsia/Eclampsia | 3.4 (3.2–3.6) |
| Cesarean delivery | 1.4 (1.1–1.7) |
| **Stillbirth** | **16.5 (2.9–92.1)** |
| Fetal loss | 7.6 (4.8–11.9) |
| Preterm birth | 2.3 (1.8–3.1) |
| SGA infants | 2.5 (1.4–4.5) |
| LBW infants | 4.8 (3.7–6.3) |
| NICU admission | 2.8 (2.3–3.4) |

*CI = confidence interval; LBW = low birth weight; NICU = neonatal intensive care unit; SGA = small for gestational age.*

- Outcomes much improved; favorable for inactive or mild/moderate disease. **Confined cutaneous lupus: usually no adverse outcome.** **SLE newly diagnosed in pregnancy tends to be severe.**
- **Best outcome when:**
  1. **Lupus quiescent ≥6 months before conception**
  2. **No lupus nephritis** (proteinuria or renal dysfunction)
  3. **No APS or lupus anticoagulant**
  4. **No superimposed preeclampsia**

### Lupus nephritis
- Active nephritis → adverse outcomes (higher histological class → higher risk); much better if in remission.
- Preeclampsia **27% with pre-existing renal disease vs 16% without**.
- 309 pregnancies: **flare 30%** (40% of these with renal insufficiency); **maternal mortality 1.3%**; ⅓ preterm (later cohort).
- **Continue immunosuppression.** Permissible: **hydroxychloroquine, cyclosporine, azathioprine, tacrolimus**.
- **New or severe renal flare → tacrolimus** ± IV corticosteroids, other immunosuppressants, IVIG.

### Lupus flare vs preeclampsia
- **Chronic hypertension in up to 30%** of SLE pregnancies; preeclampsia common, **earlier and more frequent with nephritis or APAs**.
- Both: hypertension, proteinuria, edema, worsening renal function. **Management differs: flare → immunosuppression; severe preeclampsia → delivery.**
- May be impossible to separate if the kidney is the only organ involved. Helpful tests: **uric acid, complement (C3/C4), dsDNA titers**. CNS lupus can cause **seizures mimicking eclampsia**.

| Feature | Lupus nephritis flare | Preeclampsia |
|---|---|---|
| Complement (C3, C4) | **Falling/low** | Normal (↑ in pregnancy) |
| Anti-dsDNA | **Rising** | Unchanged |
| Uric acid | Usually normal | Often ↑ |
| Other organs | Rash, arthritis, serositis, active sediment | HELLP features, headache, visual symptoms |
| Treatment | **Immunosuppression** | **Delivery** |

> **Pearl:** active urinary sediment (RBC casts) favors lupus nephritis; rising transaminases with low platelets favor HELLP.

### Management during pregnancy
- **Baseline:** 24-h urine protein, serum creatinine, CBC → repeat **each trimester** (monthly if symptoms).
- **C3, C4 and dsDNA** at the first visit and with symptoms. Complement normally **rises** in pregnancy; low/falling C3/C4 suggests activity, but normal/high levels don't exclude a flare. **Falling complement + rising dsDNA with symptoms = flare.**
- **Screen for antiphospholipid antibodies** and **anti-SS-A (Ro) / anti-SS-B (La)** at the start of pregnancy.
- SLE hemolysis: Coombs+, anemia, reticulocytosis, unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia. **Early chronic thrombocytopenia → APAs**; later → preeclampsia.
- **Fetus:** **sonography every 4 wk from 24 wk** (growth, oligohydramnios); **antepartum testing in the 3rd trimester**. Allow to reach term unless compromise.

### Pharmacological treatment

| Drug | Pregnancy use |
|---|---|
| NSAIDs | Arthralgia/serositis; **avoid chronic or large intermittent doses** (oligohydramnios, ductus closure) |
| **Hydroxychloroquine** | **Continue** (stopping precipitates flares); no malformations; start in 1st trimester — **↓ SGA 85%**; **↓ congenital heart block 50%** with anti-SSA; **only treatment that ↓ neonatal lupus** |
| Low-dose aspirin | Throughout gestation |
| **Corticosteroids** | Severe disease: **prednisone 1–2 mg/kg/d** or **methylprednisolone 1000 mg IV over 90 min daily × 3 d**, then taper to **10–15 mg every morning**; risk of GDM |
| **Stress-dose steroids** | Chronic/recent steroid users in labor: **hydrocortisone 100 mg IV q8h × 48 h** |
| Azathioprine | Good safety record; **2 mg/kg/d** |
| Tacrolimus, rituximab, IVIG | Nephritis or steroid-resistant disease |
| ❌ **Mycophenolate, methotrexate, cyclophosphamide** | **Teratogenic — avoid** (cyclophosphamide can be considered in 2nd/3rd trimester for severe disease) |
| ❌ ACE inhibitors / ARBs | Switch to **nifedipine/CCB, methyldopa or labetalol** |

- No cure; complete remission rare. **~¼ have mild disease** (painful, fatiguing but not life-threatening).

### Perinatal outcome
- ↑ Preterm birth, FGR, stillbirth, neonatal lupus. Worse with active disease, significant proteinuria, renal impairment, chronic hypertension, preeclampsia, **neuropsychiatric lupus**.
- Mechanism: **decidual vasculopathy with placental infarction** and ↓ perfusion.

### Neonatal lupus syndrome
- **Lupus dermatitis**, hematological/systemic abnormalities, occasionally **congenital heart block**. Due to **anti-SS-A and SS-B** (present in **up to 50%** of women with SLE).
- **Skin lesions in 30–40%**, appearing at **4–6 weeks**; thrombocytopenia and hepatic involvement in 5–10%. (128 infants: cutaneous 16%, hematological/transaminase abnormalities 26%.)
- **Skin, thrombocytopenia and hemolysis are transient** (clear in months).
- **Recurrence in later children up to 25%.** **Hydroxychloroquine is the only maternal treatment that ↓ risk.**

### Congenital heart block
- **Myocarditis and fibrosis between the AV node and bundle of His**; almost exclusively with **anti-SS-A/SS-B**. Also with **Sjögren syndrome**.
- **Risk 2–3% with antibodies → 20% if a prior child was affected.**
- **Permanent**; pacemaker usually needed; poor prognosis — **~20% of 325 affected infants died** (⅓ of these stillborn).
- **Maternal corticosteroids, plasma exchange or IVIG do NOT prevent it.** Hydroxychloroquine is associated with ↓ incidence (especially after a prior affected child).
- **Fetal cardiac monitoring 18–26 wk** in antibody-positive women.
- Steroid treatment of established block is controversial (no RCTs). Observational: no block in 26 neonates exposed to maternal steroids **before 16 wk**, vs 15 of 61 when steroids started after 16 wk.

> **Memory hook:** "Ro/La → Rash at 4–6 weeks (transient) and Rhythm block (permanent)." Heart block risk **2–3%**, **20%** after an affected sibling.

### Long-term prognosis and contraception
- **Survival: 95% at 5 yr, 90% at 10 yr, 78% at 20 yr.** Deaths: infection, flares, end-organ failure, hypertension, stroke, CVD.
- **Combined OCs did not ↑ flares** (two large trials) — ⚠️ but **APAs contraindicate estrogen**. Progestin implants/injections fine; **IUDs acceptable** (no ↑ infection with immunosuppression). Sterilization safest postpartum or when quiescent.

## 4. Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APS)

### Definition and features
- **Autoantibody-mediated acquired thrombophilia**: persistent APAs **plus** arterial/venous thrombosis **or** obstetric morbidity.
- **APAs:** **lupus anticoagulant (LAC), anticardiolipin (ACA), anti-β2 glycoprotein-I**; IgG, IgM or IgA. Most common with SLE, other CTDs and APS; low levels in some normal people.
- **Mechanisms:** (1) procoagulant activation, (2) inactivation of natural anticoagulants, (3) **complement activation**, (4) **inhibition of syncytiotrophoblast differentiation**.
- Any organ: **CNS prominent** (arterial/venous stroke, psychiatric features, MS-like); renovascular failure (hard to separate from lupus nephritis); peripheral/visceral thrombosis (e.g., mesenteric infarction).
- **Obstetric:** recurrent loss, FGR, **stillbirth, early-onset preeclampsia**, preterm birth. Treatment (aspirin + heparin + monitoring) → **live births >70%**.
- **Catastrophic APS (Asherson syndrome):** rapidly progressive thrombosis in **≥3 organs/tissues** simultaneously; cytokine storm, high mortality; trigger found in half.

### Specific antibodies
1. **Anti-β2 glycoprotein-I:** β2GP-I (apolipoprotein H) normally inhibits platelet prothrombinase and aggregation; it is **highly expressed on syncytiotrophoblast** → antibodies promote thrombosis at the placental interface.
2. **Lupus anticoagulant:** prolongs PT, PTT and **Russell viper venom time in vitro**, but is **thrombotic in vivo**. **The only APA consistently associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes.**
3. **Anticardiolipin:** against cardiolipin in mitochondrial membranes and platelets.
- Antibodies against **protein C, protein S, annexin V** (syncytiotrophoblast) exist — **testing not recommended** (nonconventional antibodies may matter in seronegative APS).

### Diagnosis (international consensus; Table 11-4)
- **≥1 clinical criterion** (vascular thrombosis or specified pregnancy morbidity) **+ laboratory criterion confirmed twice, 12 weeks apart:** medium/high-titer IgG or IgM **ACA** or **anti-β2GP-I**, or **LAC** positivity.
- **LAC tests:** PTT usually prolonged; **dilute Russell viper venom test more specific**. Only **~20%** of APS patients have LAC alone → also do **ACA ELISA**. Assays poorly standardized; large interlaboratory variation.

| Category | Features |
|---|---|
| Venous thrombosis | Thromboembolism, thrombophlebitis, **livedo reticularis** |

*HELLP = hemolysis, elevated liver enzyme levels, low platelet count. Data from Garcia, 2018; Giannakopoulos, 2013; Moutsopoulos, 2018.*

### Pregnancy and APAs
- **Up to 30% of SLE patients have APAs.** APA positivity: **~5%** of the general obstetric population; **7%** of women without autoimmune disease who have pregnancy complications.
- With APAs (especially LAC): ↑ decidual vasculopathy, placental infarction, **FGR, early-onset preeclampsia, recurrent fetal death**; also venous/arterial thrombosis, cerebral thrombosis, hemolysis, thrombocytopenia, pulmonary hypertension.
- **ACA + anti-β2GP-I together → more miscarriage** than either alone; **higher titers → worse outcomes**.
- **Pathophysiology:** platelet damage/aggregation; APA injury to endothelial/syncytiotrophoblast membranes via β2GP-I or **annexin V** → exposed basement membrane → thrombus; ↓ decidual PGE₂; ↓ protein C/S activity; **tissue factor pathway activation**; **placental complement activation**. Not explained by thrombosis alone — **inflammation "second hit"**.

### Adverse outcomes
- **Fetal deaths are more characteristic of APS than first-trimester miscarriage.**
- Stillbirth case-control (582 vs 1547): **3–5× stillbirth** with ↑ ACA and anti-β2GP-I.
- **Worse outcomes with:** (1) **all three APAs (triple positive)**, (2) comorbid SLE/autoimmune disease, (3) **prior thrombosis and pregnancy morbidity**.
- Pregnancy failure probability **93% with ≥2 APA types vs 63% with one**.

### Thrombosis prevention
- APA titers reported as GPL/MPL/APL units: negative, low, medium, high positive.
- **Prior thrombosis + APAs → prophylactic anticoagulation (heparin) throughout pregnancy and 6 weeks postpartum** (ACOG).
- **No prior thrombosis:** options vary (Table 55-5) — close observation ± prophylactic or intermediate-dose heparin, then **postpartum anticoagulation for 6 weeks**.
  - Some treat if **medium/high-titer ACA or LAC + unexplained prior 2nd/3rd-trimester fetal death**, or recurrent early loss with medium/high titers.
- **Serial growth scans + 3rd-trimester antepartum testing.**

### Specific therapy
- **Low-dose aspirin 60–80 mg/d:** blocks thromboxane A₂ while sparing prostacyclin. Only side effect: slight surgical small-vessel bleeding. **Doesn't help APAs without full APS**; recommended for **SLE or APS**.
- **Heparin:**
  - **UFH 5000–10,000 units SC q12h** or **enoxaparin 40 mg daily**.
  - With therapeutic dosing, **measure heparin (anti-Xa) levels** — LAC distorts clotting tests.
  - Prevents venous/arterial and **microcirculatory (decidual–trophoblastic)** thrombosis; binds β2GP-I on syncytiotrophoblast, blocking antibody binding; binds APAs.
  - Complications: bleeding, thrombocytopenia, **osteopenia/osteoporosis**.
- **Aspirin + heparin = most effective regimen**, yet **~30% still fail**.
- **Corticosteroids: NOT for primary APS**; used for SLE/secondary APS at the lowest effective dose.
- **IVIG:** controversial; for CAPS, HIT, or failed first-line therapy with preeclampsia/FGR. **0.4 g/kg/d × 5 d (2 g/kg) monthly, or 1 g/kg monthly**; >$10,000/course; no benefit added to aspirin + LMWH; no ↑ live birth in recurrent loss (Cochrane).
- **Hydroxychloroquine + aspirin** (SLE): may ↓ thrombosis and improve outcomes in APS (investigational for APS alone).
- **Pravastatin** added to aspirin + LMWH improved placental flow and outcomes in 21 women with FGR/preeclampsia — needs larger trials.
- **CAPS: full anticoagulation + high-dose corticosteroids + plasma exchange and/or IVIG.**

### Treatment efficacy
- Untreated APS (especially LAC+) → frequent fetal loss; **even treated, recurrent fetal loss 20–30%**.
- Some women with SLE + APAs, even LAC with prior losses, have live births untreated.

### Neonatal effects
- **Up to 30% of newborns** have passively acquired APAs.
- Reported: ↑ learning disabilities, **4× perinatal stroke**; European registry (141): preterm 16%, LBW 17%, later behavioral problems 4%, **no neonatal thrombosis**; autism in 3 of 36 (all with persistent neonatal anti-β2GP-I IgG).

## 5. Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)

### Features
- T-cell cytokine-driven **inflammatory synovitis of peripheral joints** → cartilage destruction, erosions, deformity; pain worse with movement.
- Extraarticular: **rheumatoid nodules, vasculitis, pleuropulmonary disease**; fatigue, anorexia, depression.
- **Prevalence 0.5–1%; F:M = 3:1; peak onset 25–55 yr.** Heritability 15–30%; >30 loci; **HLA-DR4, HLA-DRB1**.
- **Pregnancy protects against developing RA** (HLA-disparate fetal microchimerism).

### Management
- Goals: pain relief, ↓ inflammation, joint protection, function (physical/occupational therapy).
- **NSAIDs** (COX-1 and COX-2): in pregnancy — small ↑ **cardiac malformations** (1st trimester), early miscarriage, **ductus arteriosus constriction, neonatal pulmonary hypertension**; also gastritis/bleeding. They **don't slow disease**.
- **Glucocorticoids:** **prednisone 7.5 mg/d** for the first 2 yr of active disease ↓ erosions. **Flares: sulfasalazine and hydroxychloroquine (safe) + prednisone 7.5–20 mg/d.**
- **DMARDs:** **methotrexate** is preferred outside pregnancy — ❌ **methotrexate and leflunomide are contraindicated in pregnancy**. ¼ of women with RA took a DMARD within 6 months of conception; 2.9% had unplanned methotrexate exposure.
- **Biologic DMARDs (TNF-α inhibitors):** infliximab, adalimumab, golimumab, certolizumab, etanercept — limited data but **appear safe** (300 exposures, no fetal effects; adalimumab 74 women no risk). Little known about anakinra, rituximab, abatacept, tocilizumab, tofacitinib.

### Pregnancy and RA
- **Improves in up to 70%** (regulatory T cells, pregnancy-associated α2-glycoprotein, **maternal–fetal HLA class II disparity**).
- ⚠️ **Postpartum flare in 40–50%.** New-onset RA risk **6× in the first 3 months postpartum**; breastfeeding >12 months ↓ later RA.
- **Fertility ↓**; ↑ hypertensive disease, preterm birth, hemorrhage, FGR (~32,000 women).
- **Cervical spine involvement:** subluxation risk (joint laxity) → ⚠️ **intubation concerns**.

### Juvenile rheumatoid arthritis
- Most common chronic arthritis of childhood; persists into adulthood.
- Pregnancy: usually quiescent; **postpartum flares common**. Joint deformities: **15 of 20 cesareans for contracted pelvis or joint prostheses**.
- ↑ Preterm birth (± FGR, preeclampsia); fetal development normal. Early disease severity predicts preterm birth and FGR; low 1st-trimester activity predicts low 3rd-trimester activity.

## 6. Systemic Sclerosis (Scleroderma)

- Microvascular damage + immune activation + **excess collagen** in skin, lungs, heart, GI tract, kidneys. **F:M = 5:1; age 30–50.**
- **Microchimerism:** Y-chromosome DNA in almost half of affected women vs 4% of controls; incidence ↑ in years after childbirth.

### Clinical course
| Form | Features |
|---|---|
| **Limited cutaneous** (e.g., **CREST**: Calcinosis, Raynaud, Esophageal dysmotility, Sclerodactyly, Telangiectasia) | More benign, slow |
| **Diffuse cutaneous** | Rapid skin fibrosis → GI fibrosis (distal esophagus), lungs, kidneys |

- **ANA in 95%**; **Raynaud phenomenon in 95%**; esophageal symptoms (fullness, epigastric burning) in half; **pulmonary hypertension in 15%**.
- **10-yr survival 70% with pulmonary fibrosis; pulmonary arterial hypertension = main cause of death.**
- **Scleroderma renal crisis** (obliterative renal cortical vasculopathy) in **up to ¼** → renal failure + malignant hypertension. ACE inhibitors may be needed despite teratogenicity.
- Pulmonary hypertension → bosentan or sildenafil.
- **Overlap syndrome**; **mixed CTD** (SLE + SSc + polymyositis + RA features, **high anti-RNP**) = undifferentiated CTD.

### Pregnancy
- **~1 in 22,000 pregnancies.** Stable if baseline function good.
- **Dysphagia and reflux worsen** (loss of peristalsis in the lower ⅔ of the esophagus).
- Renal insufficiency/malignant hypertension → ↑ superimposed preeclampsia. **Rapidly worsening renal or cardiac disease → consider termination.**
- ⚠️ **Renal crisis: uncommon but life-threatening, and does NOT improve with delivery.**
- **Pulmonary hypertension usually contraindicates pregnancy.**
- **Delivery:** vaginal unless soft-tissue thickening causes dystocia. **Difficult intubation (small mouth opening) + aspiration risk → neuraxial analgesia preferred.** Warm room, fluids, blankets, socks and gloves (Raynaud). Stress-dose steroids if used.
- **Outcomes:** ↑ preterm birth, FGR, VLBW, miscarriage, gestational hypertension (decidual vasculopathy, acute atherosis, infarcts). Diffuse disease in 45% of 214; renal crisis in 3.
- **Subfertility.** Avoid hormonal (especially combined) contraception with pulmonary, cardiac or renal involvement; consider sterilization.

## 8. Inflammatory Myopathies
- Prevalence **14–32 per 100,000**. **Polymyositis, dermatomyositis, inclusion-body myositis** → progressive (asymmetrical) weakness.
- **Dermatomyositis = characteristic rash** with/before weakness. ↑ Muscle enzymes, abnormal EMG; **biopsy confirms** (perivascular/perimysial infiltrates).
- ⚠️ **~15% of adults with dermatomyositis have a malignancy** (breast, lung, stomach, **ovary**); may be years apart.
- Treatment: **high-dose corticosteroids**, azathioprine or methotrexate, IVIG.
- **Pregnancy:** 17 gravidas: hypertension 23%, antepartum hemorrhage 11%, **cesarean 88%**, preterm 35%. In 98 women, **80% had no disease worsening**. Outcome tracks disease activity; **new-onset disease in pregnancy is aggressive**.

## 9. Hereditary Connective Tissue Disorders
- Mutations in structural proteins (fibrillar **collagen types I, II, III**, elastin, proteoglycans). Main pregnancy concern: **aortic aneurysm/dissection**.

| Disorder | Genetics / prevalence | Pregnancy risks and management |
|---|---|---|
| **Marfan syndrome** | **Autosomal dominant**; **1 in 3000–5000**; both sexes; **FBN1 (fibrillin) on 15q21**, high mutation rate; PGT/prenatal diagnosis possible in the **80%** with a known mutation | Aortic media degeneration → **aortic dilation/dissection, more common in pregnancy**; Marfan underlay **65%** of aortic dissections in 27 pregnant women (see Ch 52) |
| **Ehlers-Danlos syndrome** | **7 per 100,000**; AD, AR or X-linked; types I–III AD (~30% each); collagen/procollagen defects | **PPROM, preterm birth, APH/PPH, previa, cervical insufficiency, uterine rupture**; fragile tissue complicates repair and cesarean; **type IV (vascular): great-vessel and uterine rupture**; iliac artery rupture deaths; neonatal skull fractures (type VIIC) |
| **Osteogenesis imperfecta** | Type I **1 in 20,000**, type II **1 in 60,000**; up to 15 subtypes; AD, AR, sporadic; **type I = COL1A1, mildest**; **type II lethal in utero** | Blue sclerae, hearing loss, fractures (often 20–30 before pregnancy), dental defects; risks: fractures, scoliosis/restrictive lung disease, micrognathia, unstable C-spine, **uterine rupture, CPD**; ↑ APH, abruption, FGR, malformations, preterm; fetus may fracture in utero or at delivery; bisphosphonates considered |

## Key Numbers to Memorize
- Unrecognized rheumatic disease **0.3%** → preeclampsia/FGR **25%**
- SLE: **90%** women; **1 in 500** reproductive-age women; **1 in 900** births; twin concordance **25% vs 2%**; ANA **84–98%**, anti-dsDNA **62–70%**, anti-Sm **25–38%**, anti-Ro **30–49%**, antiphospholipid **21–50%**, antihistone **70%**
- SLE diagnosis: **≥4 criteria**; maternal mortality **325/100,000**; stillbirth RR **16.5**; preeclampsia RR **3.4**
- Lupus nephritis: flare **30%**, maternal mortality **1.3%**; chronic hypertension **up to 30%**
- Hydroxychloroquine: SGA ↓ **85%**, heart block ↓ **50%**; prednisone **1–2 mg/kg**; methylprednisolone **1000 mg × 3 d**; azathioprine **2 mg/kg**; stress hydrocortisone **100 mg q8h × 48 h**
- Neonatal lupus rash **30–40%** at **4–6 wk**; recurrence **25%**; heart block **2–3%** → **20%**; mortality ~**20%**; monitor **18–26 wk**
- SLE survival **95 / 90 / 78%** at 5 / 10 / 20 years
- APS: live births with treatment **>70%**; failure despite treatment **~30%**; recurrent loss **20–30%**; APAs in **30%** of SLE and **~5%** of obstetric population; LAC alone in only **20%** of APS; confirm tests **12 wk apart**; ≥2 APA types → failure **93%** vs **63%**
- Aspirin **60–80 mg**; UFH **5000–10,000 U q12h**; enoxaparin **40 mg daily**; IVIG **2 g/kg**; anticoagulate **6 wk** postpartum; triple positive live birth **30%**
- RA: prevalence **0.5–1%**, F:M **3:1**; improves **up to 70%**; postpartum flare **40–50%**; new onset **6×** in first 3 postpartum months; prednisone **7.5–20 mg**
- Scleroderma: F:M **5:1**; **1 in 22,000** pregnancies; ANA and Raynaud **95%**; pulmonary hypertension **15%**; renal crisis **up to ¼**
- GPA: sinus **90%**, lung **85%**, GN **75%**; subglottic stenosis **¼**. PAN: hepatitis B **⅓**. Takayasu onset **<40**
- Dermatomyositis malignancy **15%**; myopathy prevalence **14–32/100,000**
- Marfan **1 in 3000–5000** (FBN1, 15q21); EDS **7/100,000**; OI type I **1 in 20,000**, type II **1 in 60,000**
